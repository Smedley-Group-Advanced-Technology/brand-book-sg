    Colour

    A near-monochrome palette: ink and navy for structure, one pale blue for light, and a tightly rationed green and red that carry meaning rather than decoration.

    Core

    The spine of the system

    Everything structural is built from Ink, Deep Navy and Pale Blue. The navies all derive from Steel; Mist and Pale Blue are the light that falls on them.

    Surface and neutral

    The canvas, panels and quiet text

    The ground is Canvas, never pure white. White is for type reversed out of the gradient and for photo plates.

    Signal

    Used sparingly, always in pairs

    Signal colours never appear as a surface, a background or a chart fill: only as short bold labels on their own tint. The palette carries no red, orange or yellow outside the negative pair.

    On screen

    How the palette becomes two themes

    The light theme is the default: Canvas ground, Ink type, Slate and Steel for quieter text, Pale Blue hairlines. The dark theme sets the same parts on #10141A. Anything drawn in the signature surface (cards, menus, dialogs, toasts) carries the dark theme wherever it sits.

    PairingUse, contrast
    Ink on CanvasTitles and body copy. 18.4 : 1
    Slate and Steel on CanvasSecondary copy and quiet labels. 9.4 : 1 and 6.8 : 1
    White on the gradientCards, the primary action, the strapline band. 14.5 : 1 at the navy end
    Negative on its tintProblems, errors. 5.3 : 1
    #007A3D on the positive tintGains on screen; #00B050 is too light for small type. 4.6 : 1
    NeverMist or Caption for text on Canvas (2.6 : 1 and 2.3 : 1), signal colours as fills

    Voice

    We write the way a good engineer explains a result: the point first, specific, calm. Plain words, full sentences, and numbers that mean something.

    Point first

    Lead with the conclusion. The reader should be able to stop after the first sentence and still act.

    Specific

    Numbers with units, names with places, dates in full. 0.18 s, not nearly there. FKL Łódź, not a hub. 26 September 2026, not 26/09.

    Calm

    Confidence comes from facts, not volume. Sentence case, no exclamation marks, no capitals for emphasis, no hype words.

    Gradients and surfaces

    Everything dark in this system is the same 45° gradient with the same edge light. That single decision is what makes cards, tiles, circles and bands read as one object. The ground stays flat Canvas.

    The signature surface

    One fill for everything dark

    Angle45°, top-left to bottom-right
    StopsInk #000000 at 0 % to Deep Navy #222A35 at 100 %
    CSSlinear-gradient(135deg,#000 0%,#222A35 100%)
    In the bookvar(--sg-surface), or class .sg-surface with the edge light

    One fill does all the work: cards, icon tiles, the intelligence circle, the strapline band and the cover. Because it is angular rather than radial, shapes of any proportion sit together without the gradient breaking.

    Edge light

    A 1 px stroke, pale at the top, dark at the foot

    • #8497B0 Mist, at the top
    • #ABC0E4 Pale Blue, at 53 %
    • #333F50 Slate, at the foot
    Stroke0.5 pt on slides, 1 px on screen
    CSSlinear-gradient(180deg,#8497B0 0%,#ABC0E4 53%,#333F50 100%), var(--sg-edge)

    It reads as a single light source above the page and is what separates a card from the canvas. No shadow, glow or bevel does that job.

    What it makes

    Cards, tiles and the one circle, all in the same fill

    Timing dataLaps, sectors,
    split times
    TelemetrySpeed, throttle,
    brake pressure

    Anything drawn in the surface takes class sg-dark, so the text, fields and buttons inside it read in the dark theme. The circle marks the centre of a process, once per view at most; it is the only surface that is not a rounded rectangle.

    Divider line

    Pale Blue, solid to 83 %, then fading to nothing

    Data inputsWhat we collect

    A hairline that holds solid for 83 % of its length, then fades to zero. It sits under every section label and never closes a box: the fade is the point. Use .shead for a section header and hr.fade on its own.

    Strapline band

    The conclusion, dark or light

    This is moneyball applied to motorsports

    Dark: the signature surface with white Sora SemiBold, centred. On a slide it runs full bleed, 0.45 in tall at y 4.37 in, in 16 pt.

    Our model isolates performance from contextual factors

    Light: solid Cloud #D6DCE5 with black type, 11 pt on a slide. Used when the area above is already dense with dark cards, so the band recedes instead of competing.

    Every slide or view carries at most one band. It is the conclusion, not a caption.

    NeverA gradient at any angle but 45°, a radial gradient or glow, a tinted wash behind content, drop shadows or bevels, a second dark fill

    In practice

    Drawings and data

    Where something is measured, built or read, the system draws it plainly. Technical drawings sit on the Canvas in ink, measured in Steel; values are Sora with tabular figures; and green and red appear only where a number has gone up or down. The same rules serve a kart, a sensor housing and a results table.

    Drawings

    The drawing ground

    Canvas, or the signature surface when reversed

    Drawings sit on the page's own ground, with no grid behind them. The part is drawn in Ink, set out in Pale Blue hairlines and measured in Steel. Reversed on the signature surface, the part turns white and the measurements Pale Blue. Labels and values are set in Sora; a value that needs attention is set in the negative red, as text and nothing more.

    Every drawing carries a title block: sheet, subject, place and date in ruled cells. Dimensions end in short ticks at 45 degrees, or straight ones, never arrowheads.

    On Canvas, reversedLineUse
    Object lineInk, 1.25 px; white when reversed. What exists: the outline of the part.
    Construction linePale Blue, 0.75 px. How it was set out: modules, envelopes, extensions.
    Centre lineSteel, long and short dash; Pale Blue when reversed. Axes and symmetry.
    Hidden or plannedSlate, dashed; Cloud when reversed. What is out of sight or not built yet.
    DimensionSteel; Pale Blue when reversed. Short ticks at 45 degrees, or straight, never arrowheads. The value in Sora above the line.
    CalloutA dot on the part, a shelf to the label in the text colour. Balloons name items in a set.

    In practice

    Motion

    Motion brakes: fast in, hard stop, like a car arriving on its marks. Each view has one moment and no more. The logo's moment is the hero: three bars drawn in turn on the signature surface, then the lockup and the divider.

    1. 1The surface

      The tile arrives over 0.9 s: it rises a short way and grows from 96 % to full size on the brake, the 45 degree gradient inside its edge light.

    2. 2The bars

      The three bars draw in white, one after another, each from its upper end down the lean: the top bar at 0.35 s, the long bar at 0.55 s, the bottom bar at 0.75 s, 0.9 s each.

    3. 3The lockup

      At 1.2 s the lockup rises into place below the tile over 0.8 s: Smedley Group, the divider and the division name, as one piece.

    4. 4The divider

      At 1.5 s the Pale Blue hairline grows from the left over 1 s, solid to 83 % and fading after: the divider that opens every section.

    5. 5Stillness

      Everything is in place by 2.5 s and nothing moves again. The sequence plays once; Replay runs it on request.

    6. 6The press

      A pressed control scales to 98 % for 80 ms and comes back. No streak, no sheen, no light passing across it.

    Brakecubic-bezier(.16, 1, .3, 1), for anything arriving
    Arrive0.8 to 0.9 s; parts that belong together follow 0.2 s apart
    PressScale to 98 % for 80 ms, then back
    Progress fill1.4 s, quint-out, bar by bar
    One moment per viewNo loops outside loaders, no blinking, no hover choreography, no streaks or wipes
    Reduced motionShow the end state at once

    The glyph as progress

    The three bars are the three rungs of the ladder: FAT Karting League, Formula 4, Formula 1. One value runs through them in order, so a driver completes the whole mark only on reaching F1. The same three bars are the loader.

    The loader

    For waits without a known length: the three bars brighten in turn, bottom to top, in the text colour. It is the one loop the system allows. With reduced motion the bars hold still.

    When to use it

    The gauge shows progress along the driver pathway only. For a task with a known length use a progress bar; for steps use the steps pattern. Label the value in words and as a percentage.

    Interface

    Controls

    Everything a person presses, types into or chooses. Red means active, the 55° cut marks the choice, and nothing hides in a box it does not need.

    Actions

    Buttons

    One Race Red primary per view. Secondary is a 10 % tint of the ink. Text buttons for low-weight actions.

    States and sizes

    Destructive actions use the yellow flag as text on its tint. Red is never a warning.

    Icon buttons and loading

    Icon buttons take the button's cut, with the icon kept upright; a pressed one goes red. Buttons that belong together touch: their cuts run parallel 3 px apart, so the group reads as one bar. The loader is the glyph, its bars lighting Flame in turn. Loaders are the only looping motion in the system.

    Buttons with icons

    The icon leads the label, 16 px, 8 px gap, in the button's own colour. Workshop tools for engineering actions, plain strokes for everything else. A trailing arrow only ever means go on.

    Inputs

    In practice

    Principles

    The handful of rules that keep the system coherent when someone new builds the next screen.

    A pure groundFlat black or flat white, owned by neither world. No gradients, no tints. Drawings alone sit on Blueprint.
    A clear leadUsually one bold element per view, lit rather than boxed, with calmer content around it.
    Lines, not boxesHairlines and space separate sections. Cards only for things you pick up, such as downloads.
    One cutEvery accent at 55°, leaning with the glyph.
    The logo measuresProgress toward F1 is shown by the glyph itself.
    Red leads, blue measuresRed marks the driver, the leader and the action. Blue marks the grid, the data and the model.
    Motion brakesFast in, hard stop. One moment per view.
    End on the pointWhere it helps, close with the conclusion in one sentence.
    Plain wordsSentence case, full sentences, no capital-letter labels.
    Logo is consistentKept at a steady size and position within one product or deck.
    Drawn, then builtBlueprint lines appear where something is measured or made. The grid is paper, not pattern.
    Meet at the seamRed and blue touch only across a 55° cut, or with the ground between them.
    Flags for outcomesGreen for gains, yellow for losses and errors. Red never means wrong.
    Race marks mean somethingBoards mark a place, kerbs a limit, chequers a finish.

    Avoid a third typeface, mono set as paragraphs, tools that lean against the glyph, chequers on anything unfinished, drop shadows or bevels, signal colours as fills, red for errors, red as a wash or a ground, red and blue edge to edge, coloured gradients or glows (the temperature ramp for data is the one exception), a tinted ground, cuts that lean against the glyph, blinking or looping motion, boxed heroes in running content, dotted meta strings, and a strapline that restates the title.
