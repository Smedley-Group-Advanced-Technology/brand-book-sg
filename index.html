03

Surfaces

Everything dark is the same 45° gradient under the same light. On screen that gradient is the page itself, so sections are separated by hairlines and space, not boxes.

The signature surface

linear-gradient(135deg, #000000, #222A35)

Page background on every screen. On slides it fills cards, tiles and bands. Angular, never radial, so shapes of any proportion sit together.

Stage light

Pale Blue at 9 %, behind the one hero element

A soft pool of light replaces the box. The hero sits on the page, lit, not framed. One per view.

Edge light

0.75 px, #8497B0 → #ABC0E4 at 53 % → #333F50

One light source above everything. Used on plates and slide cards, not on interface sections.

Hairline

Pale Blue, solid to 83 %, then fades

Opens every section. Interface dividers use it at 14 % opacity.

Tread

F1 full-wet print, 24 % Pale Blue

Marks what is not yet done: the empty part of a progress glyph and running progress. Never decoration.

The wet-weather tread

Inspired by Pirelli's F1 full wet tyres

Every tyre in the system wears the same tread, drawn after the Pirelli Cinturato full wet used in Formula 1: shoulder blocks split by lateral grooves and fine sipes, two channels running round the tyre, and deep swept grooves that curve back from the centre to pump water out to the sides. The two halves are staggered by half a pitch, as on the real tyre. It is the pattern a car needs when grip is hardest to find, which is the point.

The tyre carries the tread and the ground carries its print, and both point the same way: the tip of the V leads, in the direction of travel, so it cuts into the water first. The same print fills the unfinished part of the pathway glyph.

06

Motion

The mark is earned on track. Three tyres brake in a straight line, their skid marks become the bars, and the glyph leans into place. Motion always decelerates like a car on the brakes.

  1. 1Braking run

    Three tyres drop in vertically, rolling a tread print inspired by Pirelli's F1 full wets behind them. One fixed light hangs over the braking zone. Each tyre sits on three shadows, a hard contact line, a mid shadow and a wide soft one, and the softer layers swing further round as the tyre passes under the light.

  2. 2Lock

    At the start of each bar the tyres lock. The tread smears into solid skid rubber and smoke rolls off the shoulders.

  3. 3Drive off

    The tyres pull away with a puff of wheelspin. The rolling print lifts and only the three skid marks stay.

  4. 4Lean

    The upright bars turn in place to 55° and set into the glyph.

  5. 5Lockup

    The glyph slides left while the wordmark wipes in from the left, as one piece. A soft spotlight stays fixed just left of centre throughout.

  6. 6Light pass

    One slow Pale Blue sheen crosses the finished lockup. Then stillness.

Brakecubic-bezier(.16, 1, .3, 1), for anything arriving
LeanEase-out with a light overshoot, 0.9 s

Principles

The handful of rules that keep the system coherent when someone new builds the next screen.

Dark firstScreens sit on the signature surface. Canvas is for print and slides.
A clear leadUsually one bold element per view, lit rather than boxed, with calmer content around it.
Lines, not boxesHairlines and space separate sections. No card kit.
One cutEvery accent at 55°, leaning with the glyph.
The logo measuresProgress toward F1 is shown by the glyph itself.
Colour is structuralGreen and red label outcomes. They never decorate.
Motion brakesFast in, hard stop. One moment per view.
End on the pointWhere it helps, close with the conclusion in one sentence.
Plain wordsSentence case, full sentences, no capital-letter labels.
Logo is consistentKept at a steady size and position within one product or deck.

Avoid pure white backgrounds on screen, a second typeface, drop shadows or bevels, signal colours as fills, gradients at any angle but 45°, cuts that lean against the glyph, blinking or looping motion, boxed hero elements, dotted meta strings, and a strapline that restates the title.
